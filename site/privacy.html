Privacy policy: Inbox Pilot

Last updated: 2026-10-24

Inbox Pilot is a Chrome extension that lets you ask ChatGPT about your Gmail from Gmail's search bar, and have it find, read, draft and organize your mail, manage your Gmail filters, add events to your Google Calendar, and run mail rules you set up. It runs on your own ChatGPT account and reads Gmail (and, if you connect it, Google Calendar) through a Google app that you create and own. This policy explains what it handles and where that data goes.

In short: Inbox Pilot has no server. The developer never receives, stores or sees your email, your questions, the answers, or any sign-in token. The extension talks only to Google (your Gmail and Calendar) and OpenAI (your ChatGPT account). Its source code is public, so this can be checked.

What it can change in your Gmail

Only when you ask, and only with the "manage your email" access you can grant (or leave unticked) when you connect Gmail:

  • Drafts: it saves draft emails and opens them for you to review. It has no way to send email.

  • Organizing: archive, mark read or unread, star, add or remove labels, move to the inbox, or move to Trash (recoverable for 30 days). It always shows you the list of conversations first and changes nothing until you press Confirm; Undo reverses it. It cannot delete anything permanently.

  • Unsubscribe: it only shows each sender's own unsubscribe link for you to open.

  • Filters (only if you allow Gmail filters, a separate permission): it creates, replaces or deletes Gmail filters only after you tick and confirm each change, and Undo puts them back. A change can also apply a new filter to mail you already have; the card shows how many emails first. It never sets up forwarding. Google's permission for filters also covers basic settings such as your vacation reply; Inbox Pilot never reads or changes those.

What it can change in your Google Calendar

Only if you connect Calendar (optional, a separate permission), and only events you approve: when you ask, it shows a proposed event (title, time, place, and the email it came from) and adds it to your primary calendar only when you click Add. Undo removes it again. It never invites anyone, and it does not change or delete events already on your calendar.

Rules you set up (optional automation)

Each rule is something you approved ahead of time: a Gmail search, an optional yes-or-no question, and the actions you chose. While Chrome is open, Inbox Pilot checks new mail and runs scheduled rules about once a minute.

  • Emails a rule's search finds are sent to your ChatGPT account only if the rule has a question, for ChatGPT to answer yes or no with a one-line summary. ChatGPT never chooses the actions, and email text cannot change a rule.

  • The actions you chose run on matching emails: label, archive, star, mark read or important, move to Trash (recoverable for 30 days), a desktop notification, or a webhook. Every action is listed in the activity log, with Undo for changes to your mailbox.

  • Webhooks send the sender, subject, date, a Gmail link and ChatGPT's one-line summary (plus the email's text, only if you ticked that for the destination) to the webhook address you entered, such as your own home automation, a phone notification service, Discord or Slack. Chrome asks your permission for each destination's site in each browser. What that service does with it is governed by that service.

  • Sync (optional): rules, destinations, the activity log and a heartbeat for each of your browsers are kept in a hidden app-data folder in your own Google Drive that only your Inbox Pilot Google app can see. It cannot see your other Drive files. Without sync, the same data stays in this browser.

Nothing is sent anywhere else: there is no backend server, no analytics, tracking or advertising, and nothing is sent to the extension's developer. Attachments are downloaded and turned into text inside your browser; only that text goes to your ChatGPT account, and only when an answer needs it. Data is never sold or used for advertising, credit or any purpose unrelated to the extension's single purpose.

Where data is stored

  • This browser only (extension storage closed to web pages and to the script that runs in Gmail): your ChatGPT and Google sign-in tokens, the connected Gmail address, and the list of models your ChatGPT plan offers.

  • Chrome Sync (only if you use it): your Google OAuth client ID (a public identifier, not a secret) and your assistant preferences (model, answer effort, results per search, search-bar behaviour, panel size). Chrome Sync is operated by Google under Google's privacy policy.

  • Conversations exist only in the memory of the Gmail tab where you asked. Closing or reloading the tab erases them.

  • Rules and the activity log (each entry: time, rule, subject, sender, ChatGPT's one-line summary, what was done) are kept in this browser, or in your Drive's hidden app-data folder if you turned on sync. The log keeps the most recent 300 entries per browser. No email bodies are stored by the extension.

How email content is treated

Email text and attachments are written by other people, so Inbox Pilot treats them only as data: ChatGPT is instructed never to follow instructions that appear inside an email, never to let them choose what it drafts or organizes, and changes to your mailbox always wait for your confirmation.

Your control

  • Disconnect ChatGPT or Gmail any time on the settings page. Disconnecting Gmail also revokes the Google token, including Calendar access.

  • Revoke your Google app's access at myaccount.google.com/connections, and review ChatGPT sessions in ChatGPT's security settings.

  • Uninstalling the extension makes Chrome delete everything it stored in your browser.

Use of Google user data

Inbox Pilot's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Gmail and Calendar data is used only to provide the features you use (answering your questions, saving drafts you ask for, organizing you confirm, adding events you approve, and running rules you set up), is transferred only to OpenAI for the answers and rule checks you request and to webhook addresses you configure, is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not read by any person.
